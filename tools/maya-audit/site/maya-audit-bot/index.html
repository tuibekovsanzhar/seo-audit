Maya-Audit crawler

If you found Maya-Audit in your server logs, this page explains what it is.

What it is

Maya-Audit is the crawler behind Maya's technical audit. The audit checks whether a website can be reached and read by AI answer engines: whether pages load, what robots.txt allows, and whether the crawler was blocked or rate-limited. It visits a site only when a Maya user has requested an audit of that site.

How to recognize it

Requests carry this user agent (the version number may change; the token Maya-Audit will not):

Maya-Audit/0.2 (+URL of this page)

The one exception is the verification check described below: those requests use other user agents, and every one of them carries the header X-Maya-Probe: ua-differential.

What it does

  • Requests /robots.txt before anything else, and follows it (RFC 9309). It obeys a User-agent: Maya-Audit group if you have one, otherwise the * group.
  • If robots.txt returns a server error, a 429, or cannot be reached, it makes no further requests to your site.
  • Fetches your XML sitemaps and HTML pages with plain HTTP GET requests. It does not submit forms, log in, or keep cookies between pages. One exception: when an address redirects back to itself, the cookies your site sets are kept for one re-check of that address only, never stored.
  • On a small sample of pages (by default at most 20 per audit) it also loads the page in a headless browser, which runs your JavaScript, to compare what a browser shows with the raw HTML. It uses the same Maya-Audit user agent, follows robots.txt for your page resources, does not load resources from other sites, sends no requests other than GET, does not follow links, and may click tabs or "load more" buttons (never anything inside a form) to see whether content appears.
  • Sends one request at a time to your site, by default at least one second apart, and honors Crawl-delay up to 90 seconds (a longer value is honored at 90 seconds and noted in the audit report). On a fast site that answers without errors, the gap may shorten to 0.5 seconds, never below your Crawl-delay; any error or slow answer puts it back to one second for the rest of the audit.
  • If you answer 429 with a Retry-After longer than a few seconds, it stops crawling your site.
  • Each audit is capped in pages and time (by default 50 pages and 5 minutes). It may fetch /robots.txt a few times during one audit to catch intermittent errors.
  • After crawling, it fetches a small sample of pages (by default at most 30) two more times, 30 seconds apart, to measure response time and whether requests fail intermittently. It sends none of these if your site rate-limited it, and stops at the first 429.
  • Only on sites whose owner has verified ownership with Maya (a verification tag on the homepage): it also requests a small, capped sample of URLs using the user-agent strings of AI search and answer bots (for example OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot) and of a regular browser, to check whether your firewall or CDN treats them differently. Every such request carries X-Maya-Probe: ua-differential. These requests come from Maya, not from those companies. Sites that have not verified ownership never receive them.
  • Only on ownership-verified sites, and only when the operator turns it on: it may ask Google PageSpeed Insights to test up to 5 key pages, 3 times each. Those page loads come from Google's Lighthouse, not from Maya-Audit, so they do not carry the Maya-Audit user agent.
  • If the operator supplies a list of pages that AI answer engines have cited, it fetches any of those pages on the audited site that the crawl did not already reach (at most 50, robots.txt obeyed) to check they still work. Cited pages on other sites are never fetched.
  • Only when the operator of an audit turns it on (off by default): if a site being audited links to your site as its social profile (sameAs), preview image (og:image), or language version (hreflang), it may request those specific URLs on your site to check that they work. It requests your /robots.txt first and follows it, sends at most 20 such requests per audit across all linked sites, and does not crawl anything else on your site.

How to block or slow it

To block it entirely, add this to your robots.txt:

User-agent: Maya-Audit
Disallow: /

To slow it down instead:

User-agent: Maya-Audit
Crawl-delay: 10
